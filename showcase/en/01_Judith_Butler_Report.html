2. STEELMANNING

2.1. The Unassailable Academic Reconstruction of Butler's Thesis

Judith Butler’s Gender Trouble (1990) executes a radical post-structuralist genealogy of gender ontologies, fundamentally disrupting the foundational premise of second-wave feminist theory: the distinction between biological "sex" (raw, prediscursive nature) and cultural "gender" (the cultural inscription upon that nature) [P:0028], [P:0075].

Butler's steelmanned core rests upon three interconnected theoretical pillars:

  1. The Critique of the Juridical Subject of Feminism:
    Feminist politics has traditionally assumed that "women" constitutes a pre-existing, coherent, and stable subject on whose behalf political representation is sought [P:0036], [P:0040]. Butler reveals that juridical systems of power produce the very subjects they subsequently claim merely to represent. By positing "women" as a universal, cross-cultural category, feminism inadvertently reproduces the regulatory and exclusionary mechanisms of patriarchal power, policing the boundaries of legitimate womanhood and silencing internal discontinuities [P:0036].

  2. The Deconstruction of "Sex" as Prior to Gender (The Metaphysics of Substance):
    Butler dismantles the assumption that sex is an anatomical, precultural given upon which gender is subsequently overlaid [P:0075], [P:0080]. Drawing on Nietzsche’s critique of the "doer behind the deed" and Foucault's history of sexuality, Butler demonstrates that "sex" is itself an established discursive construct—a regulatory fiction produced by the compulsory heterosexual matrix to retroactively naturalize binary gender roles [P:0014], [P:0075]. There is no epistemic access to a raw biological body outside the linguistic and cultural categories that signify it.

  3. Performativity as Constitutive Iteration (Not Theatrical Performance):
    In its most sophisticated formulation, performativity is explicitly not a theatrical act of voluntary dress-up or a subjective choice made by an autonomous Cartesian ego [P:0014]. Rather:

    "If gender attributes, however, are not expressive but performative, then these attributes effectively constitute the identity they are said to express or reveal. The distinction between expression and performativeness is crucial. If gender attributes and acts, the various ways in which a body shows or produces its cultural signification, are performative, then there is no preexisting identity by which an act or attribute might be measured; there would be no true or false, real or distorted acts of gender, and the postulation of a true gender identity would be revealed as a regulatory fiction." [P:0211]

Performativity is a continuous, ritualized repetition of norms ([P:0014]) enforced under severe social, juridical, and physical sanctions ([P:0130]). The appearance of an internal "gender core" is an ongoing retroactively generated effect of these bodily gestures.

2.2. Butler's Self-Declared Scopes and Methodological Caveats

In accordance with dialectical steelmanning, Butler’s own stated boundaries and self-critiques must be acknowledged:
1. Performativity is Not Voluntary Voluntarism: Butler explicitly rejects the misconception that performativity allows an individual to wake up and freely select a gender identity from a cultural wardrobe [P:0014]. Performativity takes place within strictly policed regulatory constraints.
2. Materiality is Mediated, Not Erased: Butler does not claim that physical anatomy does not exist; she asserts that anatomical difference is rendered culturally meaningful and intelligible solely through regulatory power matrices [P:0028], [P:0075].

TRUE METHODOLOGICAL SURGERY

Having granted the formidable strength of Butler’s deconstructive architecture in the Steelman, a cold epistemic audit reveals three fatal structural fault lines that the monograph cannot reconcile within its own theoretical framework:

1. The Aporia of Agency Without a Subject

Butler adopts Nietzsche’s dictum that "there is no 'being' behind doing... the deed is everything" [P:0045], arguing that the subject does not precede the cultural field, but is entirely produced by rule-governed discursive repetition:

"The rules that govern intelligible identity... operate through repetition... The subject is not determined by the rules through which it is generated because signification is not a founding act, but rather a regulated process of repetition that both conceals itself and enforces its rules precisely through the production of substantializing effects." [P:0216]

The Fault Line: If the "I" is nothing more than an internal retroactive effect of compulsory disciplinary repetition, what or who initiates subversive repetition? What is the ontological locus of resistance? If there is no pre-existing reflexive consciousness capable of choosing to parody the norm ([P:0213]), subversion becomes either a mechanistic, random glitch in the linguistic machinery (a stochastic copying error), or Butler must covertly smuggle an autonomous, intentional Cartesian agent back into the equation through the backdoor. The text remains trapped in an unresolvable aporia between total discursive determinism and unexplained voluntaristic resistance.

2. Radical Discursive Idealism and the Erasure of Somatic Materiality

Butler collapses anatomical sex into gendered discourse:

"was always already gender, with the consequence that the distinction between sex and gender turns out to be no distinction at all... gender is also the discursive/cultural means by which 'sexed nature' or 'a natural sex' is produced and established as 'prediscursive,' prior to culture" [P:0042].

The Fault Line: By asserting that anatomical sex is entirely a regulatory fiction of compulsory heterosexuality, Butler commits an absolute linguistic reductionism (pan-textual idealism). While the cultural meaning and taxonomic classification of bodies are discursively mediated, the physical ontology of an organism—anisogamy (gamete dimorphism), chromosomal architecture, endocrine receptors, metabolic sex differences, lactation, and gestation—cannot be dissolved into grammar. Butler treats the biological body as a passive, malleable tabula rasa devoid of any intrinsic evolutionary, physiological, or somatic resistance.

3. The Parody Ambiguity: Subversion vs. Re-inscription

Butler privileges drag and theatrical camp as the preeminent vehicles for exposing the "phantasmatic" nature of gender:

"The performance of drag plays upon the distinction between the anatomy of the performer and the gender that is being performed... In imitating gender, drag implicitly reveals the imitative structure of gender itself—as well as its contingency." [P:0206], [P:0218]

The Fault Line: Parody possesses zero inherent subversive efficacy. As anthropological and historical analyses of carnival have demonstrated for centuries, institutionalized parody and ritual inversion frequently operate as conservative safety valves (cathartic containment) that reinforce, naturalize, and stabilize dominant patriarchal norms. Butler provides no epistemological or empirical criterion to differentiate between a truly transformative, politically destabilizing parody and a reactionary pastiche that merely degrades womanhood or re-inscribes sexist archetypes for spectacle.

FALLACY AUDIT

  1. Motte-and-Bailey Fallacy (Discursive Pan-Reductionism vs. Linguistic Intelligibility):
  2. Bailey (Audacious Formulation): Biological sex does not exist prior to cultural discourse; anatomy is literally an artifact of gendered power relations: "the distinction between sex and gender turns out to be no distinction at all" [P:0042].
  3. Motte (Defensive Retreat): When pressed on material reality, the author retreats to the modest claim that we simply cannot access the body without language: "the body is not denied material reality, but its intelligibility is culturally mediated" [P:0014], [P:0075]. The philosophical heavy lifting is performed by the radical Bailey, while epistemic defense relies on the banal Motte.
  4. Equivocation / Conceptual Slippage (Performativity vs. Theatrical Performance):
  5. Verbatim Citation: In [P:0014], performativity is strictly defined as an involuntary, non-theatrical ritual of iteration ("performativity is not a singular act, but a repetition and a ritual").
  6. The Slippage: Yet in [P:0206]–[P:0218], to locate political hope, Butler constantly slips into theatrical, agentic illustrations of dress-up, drag, and stylization. This semantic equivocation allows the text to oscillate between non-agentic structuralism and subjective creative empowerment at will.
  7. Petitio Principii (Begging the Question on the "Heterosexual Matrix"):
  8. Verbatim Citation: "The internal coherence or unity of either gender, man or woman, thereby requires both a stable and oppositional heterosexuality." [P:0047], [P:0084]
  9. Epistemic Verdict: Butler assumes what must be proven: that sexual dimorphism and binary gender exist solely to enforce compulsory heterosexual reproduction, ignoring evolutionary biology, sexual reproduction across animal taxa, and cross-cultural structural constants that predate modern capitalism and bourgeois heteronormativity.

  • Performativity as Discursive Socialization: The sociological insight that gender identity is acquired and stabilized through repeated cultural iteration remains a foundational contribution to social theory.
