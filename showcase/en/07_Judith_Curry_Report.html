2. STEELMAN

2.1. The Strongest Methodological Formulation of Curry's Thesis

Judith Curry’s Climate Uncertainty and Risk (2023) constitutes the most rigorous decision-theoretic and physical critique of the dominant international climate policy paradigm published by a senior climatologist [P:0025], [P:0098].

Curry’s thesis rests upon four foundational pillars:
1. The "Wicked Problem" Framing vs. False Policy Consensus: Drawing on Gwyn Prins and Steve Rayner, Curry demonstrates that climate change is fundamentally a "wicked mess"—characterized by persistent systemic ignorance, conflicting societal values, and non-linear dynamics [P:0098], [P:0099]. The 1992 UNFCCC treaty prematurely framed climate change as a "tame" pollution problem solvable by a single global regulatory dial (rapid forced elimination of CO2 emissions), generating massive framing bias and silencing legitimate scientific debate [P:0098], [P:0104].
2. Structural Model Inadequacy and CMIP6 Overheating: State-of-the-art General Circulation Models (GCMs) in CMIP6 cannot reliably predict regional or decadal-scale climate changes because of parameterized sub-grid physics, specifically cloud-radiative feedbacks and aerosol-cloud interactions [P:0149], [P:0181]. A substantial subset of CMIP6 models run excessively hot, with effective climate sensitivities exceeding 5.0°C that conflict with historical observational constraints [P:0149], [P:0182].
3. The Power of Natural Internal Variability: Observed warming since the pre-industrial era is not purely anthropogenic; multi-decadal and century-scale natural internal ocean-atmosphere circulations (e.g., AMO, PDO) account for substantial portions of observed early 20th-century warming (1910–1940) and mid-20th-century cooling (1940–1970) [P:0040], [P:0182]. Attributing all warming since 1850 to CO2 represents a fundamental attribution error that overestimates climate sensitivity [P:0040].
4. Decision-Making Under Deep Uncertainty (DMDU) & Climate Pragmatism: When uncertainties are deep and structural, optimizing policy around single-point model predictions is maladaptive [P:0016], [P:0133]. Following the Hartwell Paper and DMDU methodologies, society must abandon fragile net-zero deadlines in favor of "anti-fragile" no-regrets strategies: decentralized energy resilience, rapid advanced nuclear fission (SMRs), geothermal innovation, and adaptation to extreme weather regardless of cause [P:0498], [P:0499].

Curry summarizes this central challenge:

"Climate change is an issue that presents great scientific and economic complexity, some very deep uncertainties... A framing bias occurs when a narrow frame pre-ordains the conclusion to a much more complex problem." [P:0098].

2.2. The Author's Own Caveats and Scope Boundaries

Curry explicitly dissociates herself from crude climate denialism through clear boundary conditions:
1. Physical Reality of the Greenhouse Effect: She affirms that the earth has warmed since 1860, that atmospheric CO2 concentrations have increased primarily due to fossil fuel emissions, and that CO2 is physically an infrared-absorbing greenhouse gas [P:0040], [P:0498].
2. Rejection of Complete Climate Inaction: She explicitly states that uncertainty does not justify complacency or doing nothing; rather, it demands resilient risk hedging rather than monocultural technological mandates [P:0133], [P:0498].
3. Acknowledgment of Tail Risks: She acknowledges that extreme worst-case warming scenarios cannot be mathematically ruled out, but insists they should be managed via contingency planning rather than treated as deterministic central forecasts [P:0238], [P:0287].

TRUE METHODOLOGICAL SURGERY

Having established the maximum analytical force of Curry’s critique, the audit isolates three decisive methodological fractures where her framework falters under forensic scrutiny:

1. Asymmetric Epistemic Skepticism (Models vs. Observational Baselines)

  • Methodological Fracture: Curry exercises hyper-critical scrutiny toward climate model physics (GCM cloud parameterizations and parameterized equations) while displaying an uncritical leniency toward early observational surface temperature records [P:0040], [P:0149], [P:0182].
  • Forensic Diagnosis: Asymmetric Skepticism. To argue that early 20th-century warming (1910–1940) rivaled late 20th-century warming due to natural cycles, Curry relies on historical thermometer datasets that suffer from vast geographic sampling voids in the southern hemisphere, historical changes in sea-surface temperature bucket measurements, and urban heat island biases. If deep uncertainty cripples complex physical models, that identical deep uncertainty severely corrupts the sparse historical observations used to infer large natural oceanic oscillations.
  • Concrete Example: An auditor who demands micro-second biometric verification before accepting a digital transaction, but gladly accepts a handwritten paper receipt from 1910 with smudged ink as absolute proof of purchase, commits asymmetric skepticism. Curry holds GCM simulations to an impossible standard of complete mechanical verification while accepting imperfect early 20th-century thermometer anomalies to assert dominant natural variability.

2. Low Climate Sensitivity Anchoring and the Sherwood Synthesis

  • Methodological Fracture: Curry anchors her low-risk narrative on observationally based energy balance estimates suggesting an Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS) of 1.2°C to 2.0°C [P:0182], while treating the multi-evidence consensus range (2.6°C–3.9°C) synthesized by Sherwood et al. (2020) as an artifact of consensus bias [P:0182], [P:0197].
  • Forensic Diagnosis: Selective Anchoring & Neglect of Pattern Effects. Energy budget models that yield an ECS below 2.0°C rely on the simplistic assumption that spatial surface warming patterns remain uniform over centuries. When modern spatial "pattern effect" physics and paleoclimatic constraints (Last Glacial Maximum and Pliocene warmth) are incorporated, an ECS below 2.0°C has less than a 5% empirical probability [P:0182], [P:0191]. Anchoring risk management on an improbable lower bound directly violates her own foundational principle of robust risk hedging under deep uncertainty.
  • Concrete Example: An insurance company calculating homeowner hurricane risk based solely on the calmest five-year period in history, while dismissing thirty years of regional storm physics as "overheated models," violates fiduciary duty. By hedging against the high costs of decarbonization while minimizing the irreversible fat-tail risks of climate sensitivity, Curry creates an asymmetric vulnerability.

3. The Implementation Lag of "Climate Pragmatism" (Nuclear & SMRs)

  • Methodological Fracture: Curry proposes that society should slow down or avoid aggressive near-term mandates on wind and solar power, relying instead on next-generation advanced nuclear reactors (SMRs), deep geothermal, and future energy breakthroughs [P:0498], [P:0499].
  • Forensic Diagnosis: Technological Replacement Fallacy & Temporal Risk Mismatch. While small modular reactors and advanced nuclear are technically attractive, they currently face immense regulatory hurdles, supply-chain constraints, astronomical licensing lead times, and capital cost escalations. Recommending a multi-decade pause on deploying readily available commercial renewables while waiting for speculative advanced nuclear architectures introduces catastrophic temporal risk: if climate sensitivity is 3.0°C, the delay locks in irreversible cryospheric and oceanic tipping points during the transition window.
  • Concrete Example: A ship captain who refuses to deploy existing lifeboats because a brand-new, ultra-luxurious motorized rescue vessel has been ordered from the shipyard—even though that rescue vessel will not arrive for twelve hours while the ship is taking on water—commits a fatal temporal risk mismatch.
