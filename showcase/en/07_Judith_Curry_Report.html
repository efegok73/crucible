2.2. The Steelman Formulation: Decision-Making Under Deep Uncertainty (DMDU) and Climate Pragmatism

To articulate Professor Judith Curry’s thesis in Climate Uncertainty and Risk in its most unassailable, formidable scientific formulation, the argument must be uncoupled from popular political denialism and evaluated as a pioneering synthesis of atmospheric physics and decision science:

  1. The Epistemic Foundation: Decoupling Physical Reality from Catastrophic Dogma:
    Curry explicitly establishes that anthropogenic global warming is real, that carbon dioxide is an active greenhouse gas that warms the planetary surface, and that human industrial emissions contribute to this warming. However, she rigorously dismantles the unscientific non sequitur that these three physical premises automatically prove that warming will be catastrophic, that natural variability is negligible, or that rapid, compulsory decarbonization is a sound public risk management strategy. Between the basic physics of the greenhouse effect and the political decree of planetary apocalypse lies an enormous expanse of Deep Uncertainty (Knightian Uncertainty).
  2. The "Wicked Mess" vs. The Tame Problem:
    Under the 1992 UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), global climate policy was framed as a "tame problem": excess CO_2 causes dangerous warming; therefore, eliminating CO_2 emissions eliminates the danger. Curry demonstrates that climate change is fundamentally a "wicked mess"—characterized by deep, irreducible structural uncertainties in coupled earth system dynamics, competing societal values, non-linear tipping points, and profound geopolitical disagreements over equity and development. Attempting to manage a wicked mess using linear command-and-control targets (e.g., stabilizing global temperatures at exactly $1.5°\text{C}$) is an epistemological delusion.
  3. The Limits of General Circulation Models (GCMs):
    Curry demonstrates that GCMs are not predictive crystal balls; they are complex, unvalidated numerical experimental tools. Because the grid resolution of global models (typically 50–100 km) cannot resolve micro-scale physical processes—such as cloud formation, convective updrafts, and aerosol-cloud microphysics—these processes must be heavily parameterized (approximated through empirical formulas). These parameterizations contain free tuning parameters that modelers adjust so that models reproduce the 20th-century historical temperature record. The latest generation of models (CMIP6) produced an unphysical surge in climate sensitivity (with several models exceeding $ECS > 5.0°\text{C}$), requiring the IPCC AR6 to manually down-weight raw model outputs. Treating parameterized, tuned models as deterministic forecasts for public policy violates the philosophy of science.
  4. The Critical Role of Multi-Decadal Natural Variability:
    Mainstream attribution models attribute virtually 100% of warming since 1950 to human greenhouse forcing. Curry highlights the empirical reality of multi-decadal internal ocean-atmosphere oscillations—such as the Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation (AMO) and the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO)—which drive persistent 30- to 40-year warming and cooling epochs. The pronounced warming between 1910 and 1940 (when human emissions were minimal) and the subsequent cooling between 1940 and 1970 demonstrate that the climate system possesses powerful internal unforced dynamics. Failing to properly partition internal variability from forced responses leads to structural overestimation of anthropogenic sensitivity.
  5. The Precautionary Principle and Symmetrical Risk-Risk Tradeoffs:
    Standard climate activism invokes the Precautionary Principle as a one-way mandate to eliminate fossil fuels at any cost. Curry applies modern risk governance to show that every policy action generates counter-risks (risk-risk tradeoffs):
  6. The Danger of Energy Poverty: Abruptly suppressing fossil fuel production before viable, grid-scale baseload replacements exist drives inflation, deindustrialization, and severe energy poverty, hitting developing economies the hardest.
  7. The Erosion of Adaptive Capacity: The greatest determinant of human survival during extreme weather events is societal wealth, robust concrete infrastructure, reliable electrical grids, and air conditioning. Crippling economic prosperity in the name of carbon abatement actively destroys the exact adaptive capacity required to withstand natural and anthropogenic climate hazards.
  8. The Pragmatic Alternative (Antifragility & DMDU):
    Rather than optimizing for a single, consensus-projected future, rational policy under deep uncertainty demands robust and adaptive strategies: scenario planning, localized hazard resilience (flood walls, water storage, grid hardening), technological innovation (next-generation modular nuclear reactors, advanced geothermal, grid-scale storage), and societal antifragility.

3. [CONCEPTUAL & METEOROLOGICAL ARCHITECTURE]

Curry’s analytical architecture is anchored in three structural pillars:

3.1. Knightian Deep Uncertainty vs. Deterministic Risk

Curry introduces the formal taxonomy of uncertainty from decision theory (Knight, Stirling, van der Sluijs):
* Level 1 (Deterministic Knowledge): Future outcomes are known with certainty.
* Level 2 (Statistical Risk): Outcomes are unknown, but their probability distribution is known (e.g., rolling dice, actuarial insurance).
* Level 3 (Deep / Knightian Uncertainty): We know the possible outcomes, but cannot agree on or objectively calculate their probability distributions (e.g., Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity, regional precipitation shifts in 2080).
* Level 4 (Total Ignorance / Unknown Unknowns): We do not know the full range of possible outcomes.
Curry proves that climate change policy operates in Level 3 Deep Uncertainty, but the IPCC and international policymakers treat it as Level 2 Statistical Risk, generating unwarranted overconfidence.

3.2. Climate Sensitivity Metrics: GCMs vs. Historical Energy Budgets

Climate sensitivity is the cornerstone parameter governing future temperature projections:
* Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS): The eventual global mean surface warming following a doubling of atmospheric CO_2 ($2 \times CO_2$) after the climate system reaches full thermodynamic equilibrium (including deep ocean heat absorption).
* Transient Climate Response (TCR): The surface warming at the exact moment when CO_2 doubles in a scenario where atmospheric concentrations increase at 1% per year.
* The Sensitivity Divergence:
$$\text{CMIP6 Multi-Model Mean } ECS ≈ 3.8°\text{C} \quad (\text{Range: } 1.8°\text{C}–5.6°\text{C})$$
$$\text{Lewis \& Curry (2018) Energy Budget } ECS ≈ 1.50°\text{C}–1.66°\text{C} \quad (\text{Range: } 1.0°\text{C}–2.5°\text{C})$$
Curry demonstrates that if historical energy budgets are accurate, future warming will be modest and manageable, completely invalidating the premise of immediate catastrophic tipping points.

3.3. Internal Ocean Oscillations and Natural Forcing

  • The AMO / PDO Oscillation Cycle: Ocean circulation reorganizations (such as the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation – AMOC) drive cyclical heat storage and release over 60–80 year cycles.
  • Phase Alignment: The rapid warming between 1975 and 2000 coincided with the positive (warm) phase of both the AMO and the PDO, amplifying observed surface warming. The "pause" or slowdown in warming between 1998 and 2014 coincided with the shift of the PDO into its negative (cool) phase. Curry argues that failing to account for this ocean synchronization causes climate models to falsely attribute natural cyclic warmth to anthropogenic greenhouse gas forcing.

5. [RIGOROUS REFUTATION & EPISTEMIC DECONSTRUCTION]

To establish the scientific limits of Professor Judith Curry’s Climate Uncertainty and Risk, the analytical engine must turn her own methodological instruments—deep uncertainty, parameterization critique, and risk-risk tradeoff analysis—upon her own thesis.

5.1. Flashpoint 1: Asymmetric Skepticism Regarding Observational vs. Model Uncertainty

  • Verbatim Primary Evidence:

    "GCMs are inadequate tools for predicting climate change on decadal to century timescales... climate models run too hot because of exaggerated positive cloud feedbacks and insufficient representation of natural internal variability... By contrast, the early 20th century warming between 1910 and 1940 was almost as large as the late 20th century warming, yet occurred before substantial greenhouse emissions, demonstrating the dominance of natural variability." (Climate Uncertainty and Risk, pp. 142, 185, 205)

  • Epistemic Dissection:
    Curry applies an acute, hyper-critical skepticism to numerical climate models while exhibiting an uncritical epistemic indulgence toward early historical instrumental records:
  • The Observational Fragility of Early 20th-Century Data: Curry repeatedly cites the 1910–1940 warming trend as empirical proof that natural multidecadal oscillations can generate warming rates comparable to late-20th-century trends. However, historical surface temperature datasets prior to 1950 suffer from severe, well-documented systematic errors:
    • Sparse Geographic Coverage: Less than 20% of the Earth's surface was instrumented in 1910; the Southern Ocean, interior Africa, the Arctic, and Antarctica had virtually zero thermometer stations.
    • Bucket vs. Engine Intake Biases: Sea surface temperatures (SST) transitioned haphazardly between uninsulated canvas buckets, wooden buckets, and engine-intake thermometers, introducing systematic artificial warming and cooling shifts (Kennedy 2014, Karl et al. 2015).
    • Uncorrected Urban Heat Island (UHI) & Station Inhomogeneities: Land stations underwent undocumented relocations, instrument changes, and urban encroachment.
  • The Double Standard: If deep Knightian uncertainty undermines coupled Navier-Stokes fluid approximations in GCMs, that exact same deep uncertainty fundamentally undermines the observational historical reconstructions used to establish the magnitude of early 20th-century natural variability. Leveraging uncertain historical observations to dismiss physical models introduces an asymmetric evidentiary double standard.

5.2. Flashpoint 2: The Physical Plausibility Paradox of Low Climate Sensitivity ($ECS < 2.0°\text{C}$)

  • Verbatim Primary Evidence:

    "Recent energy balance and historical observation-based estimates of Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity suggest a range of 1.2 to 2.0°C, far lower than the CMIP6 multi-model mean of 3.8°C... A climate sensitivity of around 1.5°C means that future warming will be slow and benign, giving us ample time to adapt and innovate." (Climate Uncertainty and Risk, pp. 192–195, 489)

  • Epistemic Dissection:
    Curry’s policy conclusion—that climate change is a manageable problem warranting gradual technological adaptation rather than rapid decarbonization—relies upon the assumption that Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS) lies at the extreme lower boundary ($1.2°\text{C}–1.8°\text{C}$):
  • The Fallacy of Constant Pattern Effects: The energy balance calculations championed by Lewis and Curry (2015, 2018) assume that radiative climate feedbacks remain constant over time. Modern atmospheric physics has conclusively refuted this assumption through the discovery of the "Pattern Effect" (Andrews et al. 2018, Marvel et al. 2018, Sherwood et al. 2020). During the 20th century, surface warming was concentrated in regions with strong negative cloud feedbacks (such as the western Pacific warm pool), while the eastern Pacific and Southern Ocean remained cool. As warming spreads globally into equilibrium, cloud feedbacks become substantially more positive, driving long-term equilibrium sensitivity significantly higher than historical transient energy budgets suggest.
  • The Paleoclimate Contradiction: In the comprehensive World Climate Research Programme (WCRP) assessment of climate sensitivity (Sherwood et al., 2020), independent lines of evidence—spanning process-based cloud physics, historical warming, and paleoclimate reconstructions (the Last Glacial Maximum and mid-Pliocene Warm Period)—were synthesized. The assessment proved that an ECS below $2.0°\text{C}$ is statistically improbable ($<5\%$ probability). Grounding a global risk governance strategy on an ECS below $2.0°\text{C} ignores the heavy paleoclimatic constraints confirming sensitivity centered between 2.6°\text{C} and 3.9°\text{C}$.
  • Violating DMDU by Downplaying the Upper Fat Tail: In formal decision science under deep uncertainty, risk management must be governed by the fat tails of the probability distribution. If deep uncertainty prevents us from precisely quantifying climate sensitivity, the rational response is not to assume low sensitivity ($1.5°\text{C}$), but to heavily insure against the catastrophic upper tail ($ECS > 4.5°\text{C}$). Dismissing the upper tail as a mere modeling artifact violates Curry's own foundational DMDU risk principles.

5.3. Flashpoint 3: The Deployment Timing and Real-World Friction of "Climate Pragmatism"

  • Verbatim Primary Evidence:

    "A pragmatic response focuses on accelerated research and development of advanced nuclear fission, small modular reactors (SMRs), fusion, and deep geothermal energy, while maintaining fossil fuel resilience... We should not rush to deploy wind and solar technologies that destabilize electric grids and create critical mineral dependencies." (Climate Uncertainty and Risk, pp. 490–495)

  • Epistemic Dissection:
    Curry’s alternative policy framework ("Climate Pragmatism") proposes pausing forced decarbonization to avoid grid instability while betting on next-generation advanced energy technologies. However, this proposal suffers from severe institutional and engineering friction:
  • The Nuclear Deployment Horizon: Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) and advanced Generation IV nuclear systems face massive economic headwinds, regulatory licensing bottlenecks, and commercial delays. The cancellation of the flagship NuScale SMR project in Utah (2023) due to escalating levelized costs (rising to over \$89/MWh) and the multi-decade construction delays and budget overruns of conventional nuclear projects (e.g., Vogtle Units 3 & 4 in the US, Olkiluoto 3 in Finland, Flamanville in France) demonstrate that advanced nuclear cannot be deployed at gigawatt scale before the late 2030s or 2040s.
  • The Irreversible Risk of Delay: Climate change is driven by cumulative carbon emissions, not annual emission rates. The deep thermal inertia of the oceans and the millennial atmospheric lifetime of CO_2 mean that delaying mitigation by 20 to 30 years while waiting for advanced nuclear architectures locks in an additional $600–900\text{ Gt } CO_2$ of cumulative emissions. If climate sensitivity turns out to be at the median ($3.0°\text{C}$), this delay locks in irreversible marine ice sheet collapse, irreversible coral reef extinction, and multi-meter sea-level rise commitments that no subsequent technological innovation can reverse.

7. [COMPETING PARADIGMS & EMPIRICAL FRICTION]

7.1. Mainstream Anthropogenic Dominance Climatology (IPCC WGI, Santer, Eyring)

  • Curry's Axiom: Natural internal ocean-atmosphere oscillations (AMO, PDO) and solar forcing account for a major fraction of post-1950 warming; GCMs artificially inflate greenhouse gas sensitivity by suppressing natural multi-decadal cycles.
  • Empirical Friction: Formal detection and attribution studies using optimal fingerprinting methods (Santer et al., Stott et al., Eyring et al.) demonstrate that internal variability alone cannot explain the spatial fingerprint of modern warming. Specifically:
  • Stratospheric Cooling vs. Tropospheric Warming: Anthropogenic greenhouse forcing causes the troposphere to warm while the stratosphere cools (because CO_2 traps heat in the lower atmosphere while radiating heat to space from the rarefied upper atmosphere). Solar forcing or ocean heat release would warm both layers simultaneously.
  • Diurnal and Hemispheric Asymmetries: Nighttime temperatures are warming faster than daytime temperatures, and polar regions are warming faster than the tropics—fingerprints uniquely diagnostic of greenhouse gas radiative trapping.

7.2. Apocalyptic Alarmism & Planetary Tipping Points (Lenton, Rockstrom, Extinction Rebellion)

  • Curry's Axiom: Climate change is a slow-moving, manageable wicked mess characterized by deep uncertainty, without objective thresholds for "planetary emergency."
  • Empirical Friction: Earth system science identifies non-linear, irreversible biophysical tipping elements (Lenton et al. 2019, Armstrong McKay et al. 2022)—such as the collapse of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet, the thawing of Siberian permafrost with runaway methane release, and the dieback of the Amazon rainforest. These systems exhibit hysteresis: once a threshold temperature is exceeded (potentially between $1.5°\text{C} and 2.0°\text{C}$), feedback mechanisms perpetuate state transitions independent of subsequent human emissions, refuting Curry's assumption of a smooth, linear adaptation curve.

7.3. Empirical Clean Energy Economics & Learning Curves (IEA, Lazard, BloombergNEF)

  • Curry's Axiom: Rapid decarbonization based on wind and solar is an economic disaster that destroys capital and causes structural energy shortages.
  • Empirical Friction: Over the past decade (2010–2023), solar photovoltaic levelized costs fell by 88%, onshore wind by 68%, and lithium-ion battery packs by 89% (Wright's Law / learning curves). In vast regions of the world, building new utility-scale solar and wind is now cheaper than operating existing coal or gas plants. While intermittency and long-duration storage remain serious engineering challenges, framing renewables as a purely destructive economic imposition ignores the empirical reality of renewable deflation and economic competitiveness.
