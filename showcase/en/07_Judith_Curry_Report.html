2. STEELMANNING

Reconstructed in its strongest, non-caricatured academic form, Judith A. Curry's Climate Uncertainty and Risk: Rethinking Our Response advances a risk-governance critique rather than crude greenhouse denial. Her Persona is that of a career atmospheric scientist turned private-sector weather-risk consultant ([P:0026]) advocating intellectual humility against premature policy closure.

Core Premise Architecture:
1. Conceded Radiative Baseline: Curry explicitly concedes the core physical chain: "Average global surface temperatures have overall increased since about 1860." ([P:0038]), "CO2 has infrared emission spectra, and thus acts to warm the planet." ([P:0038]), and "Humans have been adding CO2 to the atmosphere via emissions from burning fossil fuels." ([P:0038]).
2. Magnitude vs. Existence Distinction: She explicitly separates qualitative detection from quantitative attribution: "Climate change associated with increasing concentrations of atmospheric CO2 is a theory in which the basic mechanism is well understood, but whose magnitude is highly uncertain." ([P:0038]) and "The key scientific issue is not whether anthropogenic greenhouse gases have caused any increase in global temperature—the issue is how much global warming has been caused by humans, about which there is disagreement among scientists." ([P:0056]).
3. Deep Uncertainty & Complex-System Critique: Global climate exhibits "Deep uncertainty (recognized ignorance)" ([P:0133]), where "model bias (when compared with observations) is often much greater than the signals that the models attempt to predict" ([P:0161]) and "adding detail to the model can make it less accurate" ([P:0154]). Under such conditions, "science does not produce absolute certainty but is provisional and subject to change in the face of new evidence" ([P:0114]).
4. Wicked-Problem Governance & DMDU Solution: Because climate policy is "A wicked problem" ([P:0099]) where "Science is not designed to answer questions about how the world ought to be, which is the domain of politics" ([P:0112]), rigid "predict-then-act" emissions targets anchored to implausible worst-case scenarios like SSP5–8.5 (assigned a 5% baseline likelihood in expert elicitation, [P:0176]) generate severe "transition risks" ([P:0310]). Instead, "DMDU carries forward all sets of assumptions into the analysis stage" ([P:0367]), prioritizing "Reversible and flexible decisions" ([P:0369]), adaptation that already reduced normalized mortality and loss rates substantially ([P:0049], [P:0385]), and abundant reliable energy alongside long-term CO2 reductions ([P:0465]).

Author's Explicit Hedges & Scope Limits (Devil's Advocate Baseline):
Fairness requires acknowledging Curry's own stated guardrails before initiating critique:
- She declares at the outset: "This book makes no attempt to arbitrate specific scientific disputes or to prescribe policy preferences." ([P:0027]).
- She explicitly repudiates bad-faith obstructionists, categorizing "Merchants of doubt, who distort and magnify scientific uncertainties as an excuse for inaction (social skeptics)" ([P:0069]).
- She explicitly warns readers that her own 81-scenario distribution is non-probabilistic: "While each of these scenario outcomes is arguably plausible, the distribution of outcomes in Figure 8.1 does not in any way reflect the probability of the outcomes." ([P:0216]) and "focusing on a best estimate under conditions of deep uncertainty is misleading, whether it is the IPCC’s best estimate or my own." ([P:0217]).
- She explicitly notes that internal variability cuts both ways after mid-century: "the same reasoning leads to the expectation that internal variability will contribute to warming during decades in the second half of the twenty-first century." ([P:0207]).
- She explicitly concedes that the "largest uncertainty is associated with the range of temperature increases in the likely range from the SSP2–4.5 emissions scenarios" ([P:0216]) and affirms "Yes, CO2 emissions are a problem and should be reduced, but not as an urgent problem that trumps the need for abundant, reliable, and secure sources of energy for the global population." ([P:0465]).

Falsifiability Standard of the Steelman Counter-Thesis:
Her Counter-Thesis—that rapid fossil-fuel phaseouts carry greater near-term societal danger than gradual warming under realistic mid-century trajectories—remains falsifiable if her cooling skew fails to represent symmetric physical plausibility, if her preferred policy prescriptions violate her own DMDU reversibility and technology-maturity tests, or if her causal inferences rely on confounded historical and vulnerability denominators.

REAL METHODOLOGICAL SURGERY

Even after honoring every hedge above, rigorous internal inspection exposes three deep structural fractures where Curry's own epistemic rules break under her conclusions.

Fracture 1 (K4 / K7 / K9): Unilateral Cooling Skew and Asymmetric Plausibility Filtering in the 2050 Synthesis

Curry champions DMDU precisely because "DMDU carries forward all sets of assumptions into the analysis stage" ([P:0367]) and defines a plausible scenario via Possibility Theory as "the quality of a scenario to hold enough evidence to be qualified as “occurrable,” explicitly including scenarios that are not the most likely ones" ([P:0245]), distinguishing "a scenario that is improbable (<10 percent chance) versus a plausible or an implausible scenario" ([P:0238]). Her professional weather-risk rule warns that "the worst outcome is a prediction issued with a high level of confidence that turns out to be wrong; a close second is missing the possibility of an extreme event" ([P:0026]).

Yet in Chapter 8's flagship 81-scenario matrix ([P:0215]–[P:0218]), she violates every single one of those criteria asymmetrically downward:
1. Structural Truncation of Upper Tails: She removes SSP5–8.5 (because expert elicitation gave it a "5 percent chance", [P:0176], Table 7.1 82 GtCO2/yr vs 2021 36 GtCO2/yr, [P:0173]) and SSP4–6.0 (48 GtCO2/yr), leaving only SSP2–4.5 (+1.6, +2.0, +2.5°C bounds, [P:0215]). Meanwhile, she dismisses Antarctic Marine Ice Cliff Instability (MICI) as "speculative" ([P:0264]) and rejects Weitzman's fat-tailed ECS above 10°C (where Weitzman modeled 5% > 10°C and 1% > 20°C, [P:0247]) as "arguably impossible" ([P:0248]). However, in the exact same synthesis, she includes a Grand Solar Minimum that carries only "an 8 percent chance of the Sun falling into a Grand Minimum during the next 40 years" ([P:0213]), paired with speculative "known unknowns" ([P:0214]) that "solar indirect effects could amplify an anomaly in solar insolation by a factor of up to 3–7" ([P:0214]), yielding a -0.5°C solar anomaly relative to CMIP6 baseline ([P:0215]).
2. Zero-Positive Natural Variability Framing: Every natural-variability axis in her matrix is clamped to zero or negative cooling: AMO shifts [0, -0.2, -0.3°C] ([P:0207] gives "a 50 percent probability" by 2026 and expectation by 2032—leaving a non-trivial probability of persistence or warming before shifting); volcanic forcing [0, -0.17, -0.4°C] relative to CMIP6 ([P:0210], [P:0215]), omitting a low-volcanism warming baseline; and solar forcing [0, -0.2, -0.5°C] ([P:0214], [P:0215]), omitting solar-cycle maxima. As she herself writes: "All of the scenarios of natural variability considered here point in the direction of cooling through 2050, for reasons discussed in the preceding subsections." ([P:0215]). Combining the AR6 lower bound (+1.6°C) with all three extreme cooling levers produces her headline floor of "0.4°C", which she describes as "0.8°C cooler than the temperature in 2020" ([P:0216])—a claim exhibiting even a basic arithmetic mismatch (2020 warming of 1.1°C above 1851–1900 minus 0.4°C equals 0.7°C, not 0.8°C) before one even tests the joint probability of simultaneous maximum-amplitude cooling across three independent forcings.
3. Bayesian Weighting Shift Without Symmetric Updating: For Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity, IPCC AR5 gave 1.5–4.5°C (66% likely, [P:0178]) and IPCC AR6 narrowed the likely range to "2.5–4.0°C" and very likely range to "2.0 and 5.0°C" ([P:0179], using CMIP6 1.8–5.5°C models [P:0181] alongside up to 20 percent observational constraint reductions on higher emissions [P:0184], TCR 1.2–2.4°C, and TCRE 1.0–2.3°C median 1.6°C, [P:0183]). Curry leans heavily on a single critique—Lewis (2022, in press), reporting "median 2.16°C (1.75–2.7°C in the 17–83 percent likely range), and 1.55–3.2°C in the 5–95 percent very likely range" ([P:0182])—to pull her prior ↓ downward (p < 2°C > p > 2.5°C), despite explicitly noting on the exact same page that historical energy-budget estimates depend critically on the largest unresolved uncertainty: "uncertainty in the amount and composition of aerosol particles and their interactions with cloud radiative properties (the so-called aerosol indirect effect). Assumptions are also needed about ocean heat storage" ([P:0182]). That violates formal Bayesian updating (prior sensitivity should widen variance when observationally uncertain aerosol/ocean parameters are acknowledged, not asymmetrically truncate high-sensitivity likelihoods while elevating an 8% solar tail with an uncalibrated 3–7x multiplier).

Concrete Concept Example (K4/K7 — Asymmetric Tail Pruning): Imagine an aviation safety engineer auditing storm hazards across a 30-year runway plan. The engineer excludes a 5% hurricane-surge risk (SSP5–8.5 / MICI) as "too extreme and speculative to include in the design envelope," yet builds the runway drainage assuming a simultaneous 8% atmospheric anomaly amplified by an unverified 3–7x multiplier that pulls all variance exclusively toward dry weather. By pruning upper-tail hazards as "implausible" while amplifying lower-tail cooling under "possibility," the decision envelope looks prudent only because its upper wing was amputated before drawing the chart ([P:0218]: "skewed towards lower values").

Fracture 2 (K3 / K7 / K10): Selective Epistemological Asymmetry and Self-Contradictory Reliance on Weak Models and Immature Technology

Curry's normative authority rests on epistemological symmetry: exposing overconfidence in climate models ([P:0161]) and expert consensus ([P:0056], [P:0057]), while claiming neutrality ([P:0027]: "no attempt to arbitrate specific scientific disputes or to prescribe policy preferences"). In Chapters 11–14, however, she systematically reverses her own evidentiary bar depending on whether a tool supports climate mitigation or fossil/nuclear continuity:
1. Rejecting IAMs While Importing IAM/SSP Prosperity Forecasts: In Chapter 11, Curry dismantles Integrated Assessment Models used to compute the Social Cost of Carbon: "It is difficult to avoid the conclusion that the IAMs are simply too weakly justified and too varied to provide confidence in their estimates." ([P:0342]). Yet in Chapters 10 and 15, to neutralize the urgency of 2.5°C warming, she relies uncritically on those very same socioeconomic model pathways: "the IPCC’s socioeconomic pathways for the twenty-first century all have the world being better off by 2100, even under the most extreme emissions scenarios (Section 7.1)" ([P:0309]) and "Even with a continued rate of warming of 0.18oC/decade—an additional 1.4°C of warming by 2100 (totaling 2.5°C since the late nineteenth century)—all of the IPCC’s socioeconomic scenarios expect humans to be substantially better off than at present." ([P:0488]). One cannot declare economic-climate damage functions epistemically broken in [P:0342] (↓ zero confidence) and simultaneously treat their unperturbed GDP-growth projections as dependable welfare shields in [P:0309] and [P:0488] (↑ high confidence). Both outputs arise from decoupled or IAM-linked assumptions about non-catastrophic climate damages.
2. Symmetric Skepticism Turned Asymmetric ("Fairly Solid" Transition Risks vs. Deep Climate Uncertainty): Curry declares climate projections deeply uncertain ([P:0133]), yet claims: "Consequences of the transition are associated with a fairly solid knowledge base, leading many people to be more concerned about transition risks than they are about the more uncertain risks from climate change itself that are associated with a far weaker knowledge base." ([P:0310]). Notice the Consistency breach: long-run energy-system engineering, grid learning curves (2020 82% new wind/solar [P:0454]), fuel-price geopolitics (2021 83% fossil share, European wind lull 14% vs 20–26%, [P:0454]), and food-biofuel dynamics (10% grains, 58% EU rapeseed, 40% US corn ethanol, [P:0455]) over a 30–80 year horizon are also complex, path-dependent wicked socioeconomic systems—no more grounded in immutable physical conservation laws than radiative forcing.
3. Technology-Maturity & Expert-Consensus Double Standard on Nuclear Power: Curry explicitly disqualifies carbon-removal and mitigation pathways on a strict readiness rule: "Risk management that relies on un- and under-developed technologies is not a robust strategy and has a high chance of failure." ([P:0447]), and promises she will not "prescribe policy preferences" ([P:0027]). Yet in Chapter 14 she explicitly prescribes nuclear power as indispensable ("It is difficult to imagine electric power systems circa 2100 in most regions without a backbone of nuclear power", [P:0462]), appeals to an unspecified majority consensus ("Neither plant safety nor waste disposal is considered a serious risk by most energy experts", [P:0467]) after critiquing scientific consensus tactics in Chapter 2, and bets on unproven deployment timelines: "Modern designs developed over the last four decades virtually eliminate meltdown risk" ([P:0467]) and "In the United States, estimated project completion dates for advanced nuclear plants typically lie in the late 2020s and early 2030s, with plans to expand deployment further upon successful demonstration" ([P:0468]). Advanced SMRs awaiting demonstration in the "late 2020s and early 2030s" are literally pre-commercial "under-developed technologies" under [P:0447].

Concrete Concept Example (K3 — Selective Evidentiary Bar): Imagine an auditor who rejects a city's flood-damage simulation model as "too weakly justified" to merit building a seawall ([P:0342]), then cites the exact same simulation's baseline property-value growth forecast to prove the neighborhood will prosper through a 2.5-meter flood ([P:0488]), while rejecting flood-barrier pumps as "unproven" ([P:0447]) and betting the entire city grid on next-decade prototype reactors ([P:0468]).

Fracture 3 (K5 / K7 / K10): Irreversible Carbon Lock-In vs. DMDU Reversibility, Confounded Loss Baselines, and Asymmetric Bias Scrutiny

Curry anchors her risk governance in [P:0369]: "Reversible and flexible decisions are generally more robust than irreversible ones because they allow for adjusting decisions as new information becomes available, thus helping reduce regret." And in [P:0444] she acknowledges basic biogeochemical mechanism physics: "The bottom line is that there is substantial inertia in the global carbon cycle and the climate system" and "we may not see much of a beneficial change to the climate before the twenty-second century even if emissions are successfully eliminated."

Three acute causal and governance contradictions follow:
1. Irreversibility Paradox in Near-Term Gas & Deferred Mitigation ([P:0444], [P:0465], [P:0470]): Under DMDU ([P:0369]), an action whose consequences persist for centuries cannot be labeled "reversible" merely because the power plant's financial amortization period is short. Curry claims "new natural gas power plants in the 2020s can be built with the expectation that they will not become stranded assets or promote lock-in" ([P:0470]), while coupling near-term decarbonization restraint ([P:0465]) to a postulated 2020–2050 AMO cooling phase ([P:0207]). Because cumulative atmospheric CO2 operates with multi-century "substantial inertia" ([P:0444]), banking on 2020–2050 natural cooling to defer cumulative CO2 cuts commits future decades to irreversible radiative forcing precisely when she concedes internal variability will swing back into "warming during decades in the second half of the twenty-first century" ([P:0207]). You cannot turn down cumulative atmospheric CO2 like a thermostat once the AMO flips warm in 2055 (Class 1 mass-balance physics).
2. Confounder Conflation in Disaster Metrics & Asymmetric Historical Skepticism ([P:0046], [P:0049], [P:0189], [P:0385]): Curry cites real vulnerability declines—"global average mortality and economic loss rates dropping by 6.5 and nearly five times, respectively, relative to their values in 1980" ([P:0049]) and "global deaths and economic damage from weather and climate disasters dropped 80–90 percent during the last four decades, when scaled for population and gross domestic product (GDP) changes" ([P:0385])—alongside claims that "The first half of the twentieth century had more extreme weather than the second half" ([P:0046]) and "There is no observed significant trend in meteorological or hydrological droughts, no trends in extratropical storms, no trends in the total number of tropical cyclones, and no trends in tornadoes, hail, or lightning associated with severe convective storms" ([P:0189]). Two K5 causal errors emerge:
- Denominator / Adaptive Confounding: Better building codes, early warning forecasting (the very service her own firm sells, [P:0026]), medical infrastructure, and rapid GDP expansion (denominator ↑) slash normalized mortality/loss ratios even if physical hazard frequency or intensity rises—correlation between lower normalized damages and warming does not isolate stationary physical risk ([P:0292] herself notes weather becomes climate risk when events cross vulnerability thresholds!).
- Observational-Error Asymmetry: She exercises intense skepticism toward post-1950 instrumental calibrations and model tuning (1975–2000 aliasing [P:0149], [P:0161]), yet takes sparse, non-uniform pre-1950 extreme-weather baselines ([P:0046]) as reliable evidence that early-century weather was objectively worse.
3. Asymmetric Institutional & Conflict-of-Interest Auditing ([P:0026], [P:0118], [P:0471]): Curry devotes Chapter 3–4 to sociological critiques of university incentives, grant-chasing, and IPCC "manufactured consensus", yet reveals without epistemic self-interrogation that "I am now employed in the private sector as President of Climate Forecast Applications Network (CFAN)" ([P:0026]) and later remarks "In any event, the growth of renewable energy has been a substantial boon to private sector weather forecasting companies that support the energy sector" ([P:0471])—while leaning repeatedly on market-advocacy think-tank outputs in later chapters (see Layer 8) without applying equivalent institutional-bias discounting to them.

Concrete Concept Example (K5 — Denominator Confounding): Observing that emergency-room sepsis mortality per dollar of hospital revenue fell by 80–90% over four decades ([P:0385]) due to antibiotics and triage protocols proves adaptive medical capacity improved, not that pathogen virulence is stationary or safe to incubate deliberately. Likewise, 1980–2020 disaster-loss normalization shows humanity built stronger walls—not that raising cumulative radiative heat is benign.

  1. Motte-and-Bailey Retreat ([P:0027], [P:0038], [P:0056] vs. [P:0024], [P:0218], [P:0327], [P:0465]):

  4. Concrete Example: It is akin to declaring at the preface of a medical review, I offer no treatment advice, only highlight diagnostic noise, and then closing the book by telling patients that standard therapies "have little chance of improving" outcomes ([P:0024]) while recommending a competitor's drug regimen.
  5. Equivocation on Current Climate & Preindustrial Baseline ([P:0042], [P:0126]):
  6. Curry writes "Few would choose the preindustrial climate of the eighteenth century, which occurred during the Little Ice Age and was one of the coldest centuries of the last millennia" ([P:0042]), and advocates resetting risk targets so "It starts the climate change clock in the year 2000" ([P:0126]), referencing "the current climate" ([P:0126]) to which humanity has adapted.
  7. This exhibits Equivocation between using 1850–1900 as a physical accounting zero-point for cumulative planetary radiative forcing versus misrepresenting it as a normative lifestyle ideal that environmentalists wish to recreate—masking the thermodynamic rate-of-change shock (+1.1°C already absorbed by 2020, [P:0216]) behind adaptation presentism.
  8. Concrete Example: Resetting a structural load gauge to zero after the bridge has already deflected by half its design margin because traffic seems adapted to the current sag hides cumulative structural stress rather than resetting physical yield limits.
  9. Proxy Leap: Economic Losses & Extreme Counts to Total Climate Safety ([P:0045], [P:0046], [P:0049], [P:0385]):
  10. Curry leaps from "no evidence to support claims that any part of the overall increase in global economic losses from weather and climate disasters can be attributed to global warming" ([P:0046]) and lack of trend in selected storm counts ([P:0189]) to concluding that gradual warming up to 2.5°C raises minimal systemic danger ([P:0488]).
  11. This Proxy Leap bypasses slow-onset geophysical mechanisms (0.20 m observed sea-level rise 1901–2018 [P:0190], 0.2–1.0 m SSP2–4.5 projection by 2100 [P:0191], 1.8 meters interglacial threshold [P:0268]), heat-index thresholds, ocean acidification, and multi-century thermal inertia ([P:0444]).
  12. Concrete Example: Pointing to stable annual lightning strikes to argue that ocean heat content and coastal baseline rise pose negligible long-run infrastructure risk substitutes an unrelated noisy proxy for the slow, cumulative hazard.
  13. Strawman / Selective Asymmetric Equivalence ([P:0045] vs. [P:0216], [P:0245]):
  14. She emphasizes that "Every single catastrophic scenario considered by the IPCC AR5 has a rating of very unlikely or exceptionally unlikely and/or has low confidence" ([P:0045]) and "There is no actual large-scale threshold (or tipping point) in the climate that has been clearly linked to 2°C global warming" ([P:0045]), treating IPCC low-probability catastrophic ratings as grounds for policy dismissal—even though her own Possibility Theory ([P:0245], [P:0251]: "The plausible worst-case scenario is judged to be the most extreme scenario that is deemed occurrable and cannot be falsified as impossible") mandates taking seriously low-probability, non-falsified risks (8% solar Grand Minimum [P:0213]), except when those risks point toward higher warming (5% SSP5–8.5 [P:0176]).
