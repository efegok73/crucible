2. STEELMANNING

2.1. Unassailable Academic Reconstruction of the Thesis

At its strongest, Seminar XX: Encore is not a naive biological treatise about male and female anatomy, nor a conventional clinical handbook. Rather, Lacan advances a structural-linguistic reinterpretation of Freud's unresolved question ("What does woman want?", [P:0086], [P:0133]), arguing that human sexuality operates inside the impasse created when an organism inhabits spoken language (lalangue) [P:0038]–[P:0039], [P:0144]–[P:0145].

The thesis unfolds through four tightly linked theoretical propositions:

  1. Discursive Reality & The Non-Existence of the Sexual Relationship: For speaking beings, there is no raw biological instinct or natural harmony uniting the sexes outside linguistic mediation: "There's no such thing as a prediscursive reality. Every reality is founded and defined by a discourse." [P:0038]. Consequently, while biological reproduction ("fucking") obviously occurs [P:0019], [P:0127], no stable symbolic proportion or complementary formula (x R y) can inscribe "man" and "woman" as mutually completing halves [P:0015], [P:0041]. Love attempts to mask this structural gap—"What makes up for the sexual relationship is, quite precisely, love." [P:0051]—yet love remains narcissistic ("love, in its essence, is narcissistic" [P:0012]), whereas bodily jouissance of the Other is "neither necessary nor sufficient" as a sign of love [P:0010].
  2. Non-Essentialist Sexuation (∀x Φx vs. ¬∀x Φx): Refusing biological determinism, Lacan defines "man" and "woman" as logical, discursive positions available to any speaking subject [P:0077], [P:0082], [P:0086]:
  3. On the masculine side, totality (∀x Φx, submission to castration and phallic jouissance) is purchased only by positing a mythical, uncastrated exception (∃x ¬Φx, the primal father function) [P:0085]–[P:0086]. Because phallic jouissance is organ-centered ("the jouissance of the idiot", [P:0087]), a subject on this side never accesses the Other sex directly, but only through object a (the cause of desire in fantasy, $ ◇ a) [P:0078], [P:0086], [P:0132].
  4. On the feminine side, no exception exists (¬∃x ¬Φx), and the universal quantifier is barred (¬∀x Φx: "not-whole" / pas-tout) [P:0084], [P:0086]. Hence "There's no such thing as Woman, Woman with a capital W indicating the universal." [P:0078]—women exist only one by one without a unifying Platonic essence [P:0013], [P:0016]. Though fully capable of engaging phallic jouissance [P:0080], the feminine position is structurally split between the phallus (Φ) and S(Ⱥ), the signifier of a lack/incompleteness in the Other [P:0087], opening onto a non-phallic "supplementary jouissance" ([P:0079]–[P:0080]).
  5. Writing (matheme), Modal Categories, and the Borromean Knot: Recognizing that speech endlessly generates imaginary World Views and philosophical ontologies ([P:0036]–[P:0037], [P:0049]), Lacan seeks transmission via written formalization (mathemes, [P:0125]), modal logic (necessary, impossible, contingent, possible defined through writing/non-writing [P:0065], [P:0100], [P:0150]–[P:0151]), and the three-ring Borromean knot ([P:0129]–[P:0136]), in which cutting any single ring (Symbolic, Imaginary, or Real) unbinds all three without hierarchy.
  6. The Primacy of llanguage over Scientific Linguistics: In his closing chapter, Lacan reverses standard structuralism: formal grammar and communication-centered linguistics are secondary theoretical reconstructions derived from an affective, polysemic linguistic substrate he calls llanguage (lalangue) [P:0144]–[P:0145].

2.2. Author's Self-Declared Scope Limitations and Caveats

Under CRUCIBLE's Adversarial Defense Filter, the audit explicitly credits seven genuine caveats declared inside the text—none of which may be repackaged as "hidden flaws":

  1. Improvised Oral Stenography: Editor/translator Bruce Fink underscores that the book was originally "not a text at all originally, but rather a series of largely improvised talks given from notes" reconstructed from "a stenographer's faulty transcription of those talks" [P:0006].
  2. Explicit Rejection of Ontology and Closed System-Building: Responding to Jean-Luc Nancy and Philippe Lacoue-Labarthe, Lacan warns against assuming "that I have an ontology, or, what amounts to the same thing, a system" [P:0076], adding elsewhere of the unconscious that "this is not an acceptable model of the world." [P:0094] (and denying that psychoanalysis is a cosmology).
  3. Explicit Denial of Biological/Anatomical Determinism: Lacan stresses that "Any speaking being whatsoever, as is expressly formulated in Freudian theory, whether provided with the attributes of masculinity - attributes that remain to be determined - or not, is allowed to inscribe itself in this part" [P:0086], and notes of the masculine pole: "One ultimately situates oneself there by choice" [P:0077] (allowing women to situate themselves there, and men on the "not-whole" side [P:0082]).
  4. Non-Mathematizability of Psychoanalysis & Absence of Metalanguage: Despite praising mathematical mathemes, Lacan concedes that analytic inquiry is not mathematical and therefore differs from scientific discourse ([P:0123]; "If my discourse today hadn't been absolutely and entirely negative, I would tremble at having lapsed into philosophical discourse." [P:0123]), and emphasizes "There's no such thing as a metalanguage" [P:0124] because "no formalization of language is transmissible without the use of language itself." [P:0125].
  5. Linguistic Relativity of the Copula: Lacan cautions that building metaphysics on the verb "to be" is hazardous because it is "a verb that is not even, in the complete field of the diversity of languages, employed in a way we could qualify as universal" [P:0037].
  6. Non-Universalization of Supplementary Jouissance: He explicitly qualifies feminine jouissance as non-universal: "It doesn't happen (arrive) to all of them" [P:0080].
  7. Exclusion of Physiological Mechanisms: Lacan admits that psychoanalysis reveals zero physiological mechanics: "the unconscious has revealed nothing to us about the physiology of the nervous system, the process of getting a hard-on, or early ejaculation." [P:0121].

REAL METHODOLOGICAL SURGERY

Even after granting every self-declared caveat above, three acute structural fissures remain inside Encore where Lacan's arguments break down against Class 1 formal logic, mathematics, and his own stated premises:

1. Pseudo-Formal Mathematical & Logical Failures (Topology, Zeno, and Quantifier Contradictions)

Lacan repeatedly anchors his claims in formal mathematics and topology, asserting "I believe I demonstrate the strict equivalence between topology and structure." [P:0015] and "Mathematization alone reaches a real" [P:0137]. Yet when evaluated against actual mathematical and logical definitions (Class 1 rules), three concrete formal breakdowns occur inside the text:

  • Compactness-to-Countability Non Sequitur ([P:0015]–[P:0016]): Attempting to derive why women must be counted "one by one" (like Don Juan's list), Lacan defines compactness as a fault where "the intersection of everything that is enclosed therein is accepted as existing over an infinite number of sets, the result being that the intersection implies this infinite number. That is the very definition of compactness." [P:0015]. This garbles the topological finite intersection property (which characterizes compactness via closed sets having nonempty total intersection whenever every finite subfamily intersects, not intersections implying infinity). Worse, he defines open sets strictly as 1D intervals ("sets that exclude their own limits" [P:0015], glossed as being greater than one point and less than another), and from the fact that a compact space admits a finite subcover of open sets, he leaps to: "In the end, we count them one by one." [P:0016]. That inference is mathematically invalid: a finite subcover means only that finitely many open neighborhoods suffice to cover the space; the elements inside that compact space (such as points in $[0, 1]$) remain uncountably infinite. Furthermore, Lacan explicitly conflates finiteness with countability ("You may note that I did not say that they are countable." [P:0016], immediately claiming the term finite implies countability), and then reverses himself in Chapter VIII by declaring that woman's jouissance is "in the realm of the infinite" [P:0109] so as to block Aristotelian quantification!
  • Zeno's Paradox & Real Numbers ([P:0014]): To model male phallic jouissance, Lacan asserts that in Zeno's race, the tortoise's step also shrinks and "it never arrives at the limit either. It is on that basis that a number, any number whatsoever, can be defined, if it is real. A number has a limit and it is to that extent that it is infinite. It is quite clear that Achilles can only pass the tortoise - he cannot catch up with it. He only catches up with it at infinity" [P:0014]. In real analysis, real numbers are finite elements (limits of Cauchy sequences of rationals), not "infinite"; and with constant speeds ($v_A > v_T$), the geometric series of catch-up intervals converges in finite time $t = d / (v_A - v_T)$—making it mathematically impossible for Achilles to pass the tortoise without coinciding at a finite point.
  • Quantifier Contradiction & Knot-Theory Denial ([P:0085]–[P:0086], [P:0109], [P:0135]): Lacan asserts that in Aristotelian/classical logic, not-every x is Φx implies there is an x that contradicts it "on one sole condition, which is that, in the whole or the not-whole in question, we are dealing with the finite." [P:0109]. This is false in classical first-order logic, where $\neg \forall x\,\Phi(x) \leftrightarrow \exists x\,\neg \Phi(x)$ holds on all non-empty domains, finite or infinite. He then invokes "that mathematical logic which is qualified as intuitionist" [P:0109] to justify writing both $\overline{\forall x}\,\Phi x and \overline{\exists x}\,\overline{\Phi x}$ simultaneously. Yet in intuitionistic logic, $\neg \exists x\,\neg \Phi(x) bars asserting constructive counterexamples outside \Phi(x)$—whereas Lacan immediately posits positive historical exemplars (Hadewijch, St. Teresa) experiencing a jouissance "beyond the phallus" [P:0080]–[P:0082] while simultaneously claiming woman "is there in full (à plein)" in the phallic function [P:0080]. Meanwhile, on the masculine side, writing unrestricted $\forall x\,\Phi x alongside \exists x\,\neg\Phi x$ ([P:0085]–[P:0086]) is formally inconsistent without implicit domain restriction. Finally, in Chapter X, Lacan declares "But, as of our point in time today, there is no theory of knots. Currently, there is no mathematical formalization applicable to knots" [P:0135]—a statement directly contradicted by the book's own editorial apparatus at [P:0129], footnote 12, which cites Crowell and Fox's 1963 textbook Introduction to Knot Theory!

One Concept, One Concrete Example (The Museum Room vs. Dust-Speck Error): Saying that because a compact museum floor can be covered by 5 overlapping security-camera zones (a finite open subcover), the museum's visitors or dust specks can therefore be "counted one by one" confuses the number of covering blankets with the cardinality of the points beneath them. You can cover an uncountable continuum like $[0, 1] with a single finite open interval (-1, 2)$, yet you can never count its points one by one.

2. Unfalsifiable Epistemic Insulation (The Silence-Equals-Proof Loop)

Although Lacan rejects Popperian-style closed systems in passing [P:0076], Encore makes sweeping empirical-clinical assertions about women's sexual experience, female psychoanalysts, mystics, and rival philosophical critics. Rather than specifying what evidence could refute these claims, the text constructs a closed epistemic ratchet in which absence of evidence, counter-protest, and scholarly critique all function as confirmation:

  • Silence Converted into Evidence ([P:0064], [P:0081]): Lacan asserts of feminine jouissance: "There is a jouissance that is hers about which she herself perhaps knows nothing" [P:0080] ("that much she knows. She knows it, of course, when it comes" [P:0080]). When addressing the lack of corroborating clinical reports from female psychoanalysts—whom he dismisses wholesale by claiming they "haven't contributed one iota to the question of" [P:0064] feminine sexuality—he explicitly treats that alleged silence as evidence for his hypothesis: "The plausibility of what I am claiming here - namely, that woman knows nothing of this jouissance - is underscored by the fact that in all the time people have been begging them, begging them on their hands and knees - I spoke last time of women psychoanalysts - to try to tell us, not a word! We've never been able to get anything out of them." [P:0081].
  • Dissent Disqualified as Unconscious Ignorance ([P:0079]): When feminist critics or women themselves object to being symbolically excluded, Lacan preempts disagreement by asserting epistemic asymmetry: "It's just that they don't know what they're saying - that's the whole difference between them and me." [P:0079].
  • Critique Psychologized as Hatred & Obscurity Defended as Pedagogy ([P:0040], [P:0071]–[P:0073]): When Jean-Luc Nancy and Philippe Lacoue-Labarthe publish a rigorous critique (The Title of the Letter), Lacan admits he only "skimmed through" the critical final 20–30 pages [P:0072], labels their conclusions "inconsiderate", and recasts their theoretical challenge as affective animosity: "when I insistently invited you to read a book whose climax is expressly designed to discredit me" [P:0073], diagnosing them via "desupposition" of knowledge as hating him. Similarly, incomprehensibility is framed as an asset: "what is written is not to be understood." [P:0040] and "If you don't understand them, so much the better - that will give you the opportunity to explain them." [P:0040].

One Concept, One Concrete Example (The Invisible-Planet Telescope Paradox): Imagine a theorist claiming an invisible planet orbits Mars whose unique physical law is that it absorbs all light and jams every sensor aimed at it. When observatories publish empty sky scans, he declares: "Their empty scans confirm the planet's jamming power!" When astronomers publish mathematical refutations of his orbital equation, he replies: "Your skepticism merely displays hostility toward my authority." Under such rules, no observation can ever count against the thesis.

3. Linguistic Equivocation Engine & Unoperationalized Causal/Developmental Leaps

In [P:0037], Lacan perceptively warns that extrapolating universal metaphysics from the French/Indo-European copula "to be" is "a highly risky enterprise" because languages do not share identical grammatical structures [P:0037]. Yet throughout Encore, he repeatedly violates his own warning—converting local French homophonies into universal psychoanalytic axioms while making causal and developmental assertions with zero operational criteria:

  • Parisian French Puns as Structural Proofs ([P:0065]–[P:0066], [P:0091], [P:0149]): To articulate the modal status of phallic vs. Other jouissance, Lacan exploits the homophony in French third-person singular forms between falloir ("must/ought") and faillir ("fail/default"), adding faux ("false") via faux-drait [P:0065]–[P:0066]. To assert that woman is ontologically split from the philosophical "soul" and historically slandered, he leans on French phonetic echoes ("She is called woman (on la dit-femme) and defamed" [P:0091], pairing diffame with âme and infamant with femme). In Chapter XI, the master signifier S1 becomes a "signifying swarm" solely because French essaim sounds like S1 [P:0149], while S2 interrogates "them-two" via French est-ce d'eux [P:0144]. Even when intentionally foregrounding lalangue, deriving cross-cultural psychological invariants from language-specific French acoustics contradicts [P:0037]'s own linguistic relativity caveat.
  • Unoperationalized Causal & Developmental Assertions ([P:0030], [P:0062], [P:0146]–[P:0147]): Lacan declares "The signifier is the cause of jouissance." [P:0030], mapping Aristotle's material, formal, efficient, and final causes onto grammar and vocal stopping actions without defining measurable causal variables or ruling out physiological/affective confounders ([P:0121] concedes total ignorance of nervous-system mechanics). Likewise, rejecting Freud's developmental Lust-Ich/Real-Ich sequence, Lacan asserts from casual visual impressions: "Personally, I have never looked at a baby and had the sense that there was no outside world for him." [P:0062] and "From the moment he begins to speak, from that exact moment onward and not before, I can understand that there is [such a thing as] repression." [P:0062]—offering no operational test capable of distinguishing pre-verbal affect regulation from verbal repression. In Chapter XI, while making an insightful methodological point about experimenter-mediated task construction in rat mazes ("If he were not someone whose relation to knowledge is grounded in a relation to llanguage, in the inhabiting of llanguage or the cohabitation with llanguage, there would be no montage." [P:0147]), he overreaches by claiming without systematic citation that "nothing that I have been able to gather to date from this literature indicates that any such question has been raised." [P:0147].

One Concept, One Concrete Example (The "Son" vs. "Sun" Gravity Fallacy): Arguing that human males are governed by nuclear fusion because in English "son" is a homophone of "sun" mistakes an arbitrary phonetic accident in one language for a causal law of nature. French homophonies (essaim/S1, faillir/falloir, dit-femme/diffame) function as expressive poetic associations, not universal proofs of psychic structure.
