2. STEELMAN

2.1. The Strongest Methodological Formulation of Snowden's Thesis

Frank M. Snowden, Jr.’s Blacks in Antiquity (1970) provides the most comprehensive empirical defense in classical scholarship that the ancient Greco-Roman world did not possess modern biological racism or color-based caste oppression [P:0009], [P:0193].

The steelman of Snowden's thesis rests upon four empirically grounded pillars:
1. The Environmental Theory of Somatic Variation: Ancient Greek and Roman natural philosophers (from the Hippocratic corpus to Aristotle, Pliny, and Ptolemy) explained human physical diversity through climate and solar radiation rather than hereditary biological inferiority [P:0197], [P:0204]. Dark skin and woolly hair in the south, like fair skin and blue eyes in the extreme north (Scythia), were viewed as natural physiological adaptations to the sun and cold [P:0204], [P:0240]. Neither type was seen as innately superior or inferior in human essence [P:0240].
2. Absence of a Color Bar in Social Integration: In Greco-Roman civic life, Ethiopians (Aithiopes) were integrated across all strata of society: as soldiers, diplomats, athletes, actors, musicians, craftsmen, slaves, and freedmen [P:0207], [P:0242]. Unlike transatlantic chattel slavery, ancient slavery was non-racial in its legal foundations, demographic composition, and social reality; captives of all ethnicities and skin colors were enslaved, and manumission was equally accessible regardless of phenotype [P:0207], [P:0242].
3. The Literary and Mythological Canon of Ethiopian Virtue: Classical literature began not with racial derogation but with Homer’s depiction of the "blameless Ethiopians" (amumones Aithiopes), revered for their piety and justice, visited by the Olympian gods [P:0168], [P:0240]. This high valuation was maintained across centuries by Hesiod, Herodotus, Diodorus Siculus, and Heliodorus [P:0172], [P:0204], [P:0240]. Xenophanes noted that Ethiopians depict their gods black and snub-nosed just as Thracians depict theirs red-haired and blue-eyed, demonstrating cultural relativism without hierarchical derogation [P:0240].
4. The Universalist Christian Synthesis: The Early Church fathers (Origen, Jerome, Augustine) adopted the Ethiopian as the supreme symbol of Christianity's universal mission [P:0220], [P:0229], [P:0241]. Menander’s principle that natural character outweighs lineage was fulfilled in patristic theology: all nations, from the remotest Ethiopian to the Scythian, were spiritually unified in Christ [P:0241].

Snowden summarizes this foundational conclusion:

"The distinguishing mark of an Ethiopian was the color of his skin... yet antipathy because of color did not arise... The Greeks and Romans counted black peoples in." [P:0207], [P:0242].

2.2. The Author's Own Caveats and Scope Boundaries

Snowden explicitly records several vital qualifications:
1. However, he demonstrates that classical authors (such as Agatharchides) noted this childish fear ceased with maturity and was never converted into state policy or societal doctrine [P:0204].
2. Physiognomical Stereotypes: He notes that ancient physiognomical tracts (such as Pseudo-Aristotle) occasionally associated dark complexion with cowardice or craftiness, but emphasizes that the exact same tracts equally condemned excessively fair northern complexions as cowardly [P:0203].
3. Demographic Impossibility of Precision: He admits that estimating the exact numerical percentage of black people in ancient populations remains demographically impossible given the nature of surviving sources [P:0207].

TRUE METHODOLOGICAL SURGERY

Having granted the thesis its maximum scholarly force, the audit isolates three decisive methodological fractures where Snowden's analysis breaks under rigorous forensic stress:

1. Elite Selection Bias and Ideological Romanticization

  • Methodological Fracture: Snowden systematically prioritizes high philosophical ideals, epic poetry, and elite theological texts (Homer, Menander, Stoics, Origen) while minimizing pervasive popular somatic stereotyping, stage ridicule, and everyday xenophobia [P:0181], [P:0203], [P:0240].
  • Forensic Diagnosis: Selection Bias & Romanticized Exceptionalism. In popular Greco-Roman comedy, satyr plays, mime, and epigrammatic literature, Ethiopians were frequently depicted as grotesque comic figures, exotic curiosities, or symbols of uncanny alterity [P:0181], [P:0203]. Equating philosophical cosmopolitanism with everyday societal reality commits the fallacy of confusing elite normative discourse with mass social behavior.
  • Concrete Example: When a modern historian asserts that 19th-century America had no racial hostility by quoting only Ralph Waldo Emerson's transcendentalist essays while ignoring minstrel shows, popular broadsides, and street violence, they commit the identical error. Snowden quotes Homer’s "blameless Ethiopians" and Menander’s egalitarian maxims [P:0240], [P:0241], but sidelines the reality that ordinary Greeks and Romans viewed extreme physical divergence with xenophobic suspicion.

2. The Argument from Silence Regarding Daily Enslaved Subordination

  • Methodological Fracture: Snowden contends that because ancient Roman slave law did not explicitly single out skin color, color played zero role in the experience, subordination, or domestic exploitation of enslaved black persons [P:0207], [P:0242].
  • Forensic Diagnosis: Negative Proof Fallacy (Argumentum ad Ignorantiam). While Roman law was de jure non-racial, ancient Roman slaveholders were obsessed with physical marks, aesthetic commodification, and exotic status symbols [P:0203], [P:0207]. Slaves with pronounced "exotic" somatic traits (such as Ethiopian youths serving as luxury table attendants or novelty entertainers) were commodified precisely for their visible racial alterity [P:0206], [P:0208]. The absence of a formal legal color line does not prove the absence of informal, visually driven racialized subordination.
  • Concrete Example: A corporation may have formal hiring bylaws that make zero mention of social background or appearance, yet informal cultural biases determine who is assigned to high-visibility executive roles versus low-visibility manual labor. In imperial Rome, the legal neutrality of the slave code did not prevent Roman masters from exploiting somatic differences for elite domestic ostentation.

3. The Patristic Paradox: Erasure as Spiritual Equality

  • Methodological Fracture: Snowden celebrates the Early Christian patristic doctrine—summarized in the theological formula "Christ came into the world to make blacks white" (Aethiopem lavare)—as the ultimate proof of ancient racial egalitarianism [P:0222], [P:0241].
  • Forensic Diagnosis: Semantic Asymmetry & Unexamined Metaphysical Hierarchization. Snowden fails to confront the deep ideological cost of this patristic trope. In Christian patristics (from Jerome to Augustine), somatic blackness was explicitly adopted as the visible earthly metaphor for sin, demonic darkness, and spiritual blindness, while whiteness represented purity, grace, and redemption [P:0222], [P:0229], [P:0241]. Demanding that the Ethiopian be spiritually "washed white" to achieve divine acceptance is not an affirmation of blackness, but its theological negation.
  • Concrete Example: Telling a marginalized group, "We accept you fully as equals, provided you abandon your ancestral culture, language, and physical identity and become identical to us in spirit," is assimilation through symbolic erasure, not an embrace of racial plurality. The patristic church established a powerful chromatic binary (black = sin, white = virtue) that later European colonial theologians easily converted into racial hierarchy.
