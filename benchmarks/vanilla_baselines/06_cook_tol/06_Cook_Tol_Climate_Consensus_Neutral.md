## 2. CORE THESIS (STEELMAN FORMULATION)

### Primary Verbatim Passages:

#### The Cook et al. Thesis:
> *"We analyze the evolution of the scientific consensus on anthropogenic global warming (AGW) in the peer-reviewed scientific literature, examining 11 944 climate abstracts from 1991–2011 matching the topics 'global climate change' or 'global warming'. We find that 66.4% of abstracts expressed no position on AGW, 32.6% endorsed AGW, 0.7% rejected AGW and 0.3% were uncertain about the cause of global warming. Among abstracts expressing a position on AGW, 97.1% endorsed the consensus position that humans are causing global warming... In a second phase of this study, we invited authors to rate their own papers. Compared to abstract ratings, a smaller percentage of self-rated papers expressed no position on AGW (35.5%). Among self-rated papers expressing a position on AGW, 97.2% endorsed the consensus... Our analysis indicates that the number of papers rejecting the consensus on AGW is a vanishingly small proportion of the published research."* (Cook et al., 2013, p. 1)

#### The Tol Antithesis:
> *"Cook et al’s highly influential consensus study... finds different results than previous studies in the consensus literature. It omits tests for systematic differences between raters. Many abstracts are unaccounted for. The paper does not discuss the procedures used to ensure independence between the raters, to ensure that raters did not use additional information, and to ensure that later ratings were not influenced by earlier results... The results by Cook et al (2013) seem to be at the high end in the consensus literature when 'no position' is excluded, and at the low end when included... Cook’s 97% consensus is an artifact of selective exclusion, sample bias, and flawed classification."* (Tol, 2016, pp. 1–3)

### Structural Summary (Steelman):
* **Cook et al. (The Gateway Belief Model):** In the peer-reviewed scientific literature, there exists an overwhelming, near-unanimous consensus (>97%) confirming that human activities are driving contemporary global climate change. Examining 11,944 abstracts across two decades, the proportion of peer-reviewed research rejecting anthropogenic global warming (AGW) is negligible (<1%). The public perception of ongoing scientific division is an engineered disinformation campaign financed by vested industrial interests. Communicating this definitive 97% consensus operates as a vital "gateway belief," dissolving public polarization and unlocking democratic support for carbon mitigation policies.
* **Tol (Methodological Deconstruction):** The headline figure of "97%" is a statistical artifact engineered through arbitrary sample filtering, selective denominator reduction, and unblinded rater subjectivity. Two-thirds (66.4% / 7,930 abstracts) of the entire corpus took no stance on human causation because empirical climate papers routinely study physical effects, paleoclimates, or mitigation technologies without evaluating attribution. Discarding this massive neutral majority inflates an actual endorsement rate of 32.6% (and an explicit attribution rate of just 0.54%) into an illusion of 97% unanimity. Furthermore, the rating was executed by unblinded members of an activist advocacy blog (*Skeptical Science*), invalidating inter-rater independence and replication reliability.

## 3. PRIMARY EVIDENCE & DATA TYPES
* **Corpus & Bibliometric Sampling:**
  * 11,944 abstracts indexed in the ISI Web of Science published between 1991 and 2011, identified via the Boolean query: `"global climate change" OR "global warming"`.
  * Spanning 1,980 individual scientific journals and authored by 29,083 distinct researchers.
* **The 7-Tier Content Analysis Categorization Matrix:**
  1. *Explicit Endorsement with Quantification:* Quantifying human contribution as dominant ($>50\%$) $\longrightarrow$ **64 abstracts (0.54% of full corpus)**.
  2. *Explicit Endorsement without Quantification:* Explicitly stating that humans cause global warming without defining the percentage $\longrightarrow$ **922 abstracts (7.72%)**.
  3. *Implicit Endorsement:* Assuming human causation as an established premise without explicit declaration (e.g., studying greenhouse gas emission mitigation) $\longrightarrow$ **2,910 abstracts (24.36%)**.
  4. *No Position / Neutral:* Expressing no stance on anthropogenic causation $\longrightarrow$ **7,930 abstracts (66.39%)**.
  5. *Implicit Rejection:* Implying that human influence is negligible, non-dominant, or natural cycles dominate $\longrightarrow$ **54 abstracts (0.45%)**.
  6. *Explicit Rejection without Quantification:* Explicitly rejecting AGW without quantifying $\longrightarrow$ **15 abstracts (0.13%)**.
  7. *Explicit Rejection with Quantification:* Explicitly stating that human contribution is minor ($<50\%$) $\longrightarrow$ **9 abstracts (0.08%)**.
* **Author Self-Rating Survey:**
  * Direct survey invitations sent to 8,548 corresponding authors; responses collected from 1,200 authors rating 2,142 complete papers.
  * Yielded 62.7% endorsement across all papers (including "no position" at 35.5%), and 97.2% endorsement among papers expressing a stance.
* **Tol’s Statistical & Econometric Audit:**
  * Sample reconciliation tests demonstrating missing, duplicate, and non-peer-reviewed entries (op-eds, book reviews).
  * Analysis of inter-rater reliability, rater drift over time, non-independence among *Skeptical Science* raters, and residual distributional anomalies.

## 5. OPPONENTS & ALTERNATIVE PARADIGMS
* **Climate Skepticism / Contrarianism:** Paradigms asserting that natural solar cycles, ocean oscillations, and orbital forcing dominate global temperature anomalies.
* **The "Lukewarmer" Position:** Perspectives accepting that greenhouse gases cause warming, but arguing that climate sensitivity ($ECS$) is low ($\sim 1.0–1.5^\circ\text{C}$), rendering aggressive decarbonization economically disproportionate.
* **Methodological Fallibilism in Econometrics (Tol, Pielke Jr.):** Stances arguing that consensus metrics obscure scientific uncertainty regarding regional climate impacts, discount rates, and extreme weather attribution.
